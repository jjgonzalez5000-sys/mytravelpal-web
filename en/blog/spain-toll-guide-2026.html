How Spanish tolls work in 2026: routes, savings and alternatives

If you leave Madrid in 2026 heading for the Mediterranean coast, cross into France via Irun or drive south to Algeciras, sooner or later you will meet a yellow sign that reads «Peaje». The Spanish network has been changing for years: stretches that cost tens of euros until 2019 are now free, while others still charge more than €40 end to end. This guide summarises what is still in place, which alternatives make sense, and how to decide without spending the whole morning comparing maps.

Toll motorways still in operation

Most of the tolls active in 2026 are concentrated in the north-east of the peninsula and in concession stretches in Catalonia, Aragon and the north:

  • AP-7 Catalan stretch (Tarragona – La Jonquera): although the Alicante – Tarragona stretch was liberalised in 2020, the climb toward the French border is still tolled by Abertis. Driving the full 260 km costs around €22–€26 for a car.
  • AP-8 and AP-1 (Basque Country and Burgos): operated by Bidegi and AP-1. The AP-1 between Burgos and Irún keeps its main toll for trucks and international traffic.
  • AP-9 (Galicia, Ferrol – Tui): continuous toll except in urban stretches. Unavoidable if you are heading down to Portugal along the coast.
  • AP-66 (León – Campomanes): the famous «Huerna», controversial for its high per-km rate. The free alternative goes over the Puerto de Pajares and adds real driving time.
  • AP-53 and AP-6 concession stretches: Madrid’s Radial 2, the Guadarrama tunnel and the accesses to Segovia and Ávila.

Motorways liberalised between 2020 and 2024

A large part of the Mediterranean corridor and the north–south axis no longer charges tolls. Many drivers still do not know:

  • AP-7 Alicante – Tarragona (freed 1 January 2020).
  • AP-4 Seville – Cádiz (freed 1 January 2020).
  • AP-2 Zaragoza – Mediterranean (freed 1 September 2021).
  • AP-7 Tarragona – Valencia (freed 1 September 2021).

Practical translation: going from Madrid to Málaga on the A-4 has never had a toll; driving from Barcelona to Valencia by motorway hasn’t since 2021 either.

National alternatives: when they pay off

The rule of thumb most planning apps use — MyTravelPal included — is the euros-per-hour-saved ratio. Example: the AP-66 León – Asturias costs around €12 and saves 25–40 minutes compared with the N-630 over the Puerto de Pajares. With rain or fog the free road can be 60 minutes slower and the toll pays for itself; in sunny August many drivers prefer to skip the fee.

Another classic case: the AP-9 in Galicia. Taking the N-550 between Santiago and Vigo adds only 20 minutes and saves €8. If you have time, the toll is not worth it.

How you pay in 2026: Via-T, ViaVerde and Bip&Drive

Barrier-free payment (free-flow) already covers most of the Mediterranean corridor and is advancing in Catalonia. We recommend:

  • Via-T (device interoperable across Spain, France, Portugal, Italy): essential if you cross a border more than twice a year.
  • Bip&Drive or ViaVerde: same protocol, different issuers. The monthly fee (~€2) is recovered after 3–4 toll passes.

If you drive without a device, note that some free-flow Catalan tolls require you to pay online within 48 hours or you will be fined.

Restaurants and hotels at the service areas

The official rest areas on the AP-7, AP-2 and AP-68 have modernised a lot: chains such as Areas or Autogrill have refreshed the offer, and in many stretches there are B&B hotels less than 3 km from the exit. In MyTravelPal we suggest hotels beside every overnight stop — many users discover that sleeping on the outskirts of Zaragoza or León halfway through a stage is cheaper and more comfortable than pushing all the way to the destination.
