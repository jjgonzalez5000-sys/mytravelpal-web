The complete Alpine summer road-trip guide: when to leave, where to stay, recommended hotels

An Alpine road trip in July or August combines three challenges: mountain passes that open late, hotels that sell out months in advance, and traffic that on the busiest dates turns 400 km into a full day behind the wheel. With good planning, though, it is probably the European trip with the best landscape-to-effort ratio. This guide summarises what we have learned after several summers driving through the French, Swiss and Italian Alps.

When to leave

Basic rules for 2026:

  • Avoid Saturdays in August: France’s so-called «black Saturdays» are forecast on the Bison Futé calendar. 1, 3, 10 and 15 August usually gridlock the A-7 (Rhône Valley) and all accesses to Mont Blanc.
  • Leave before 7:00 or after 18:00 on days classified red. The difference is easily 3 hours.
  • High passes: wait at least until 15 June. The Col de l’Iseran (2,770 m) and the Col du Galibier (2,642 m) usually open between late May and mid-June depending on the year.
  • Book hotels in April–May. In areas like Chamonix, Cortina or Zermatt, the best value-for-money options are gone two months before August.

Classic 8-day itinerary

An itinerary tested by many testers, starting and ending in Geneva (cheap airport base) or Milan:

  1. Day 1: Geneva – Chamonix (90 km). Ride the cable car up to Aiguille du Midi in the afternoon.
  2. Day 2: Chamonix – Cortina via the Mont Blanc Tunnel and Bolzano (500 km). Long but very scenic.
  3. Day 3: Cortina and the Tre Cime di Lavaredo. Day excursion; sleep in Cortina.
  4. Day 4: Cortina – Bolzano over the Passo Sella and Passo Pordoi (140 km, 4 h with photo stops).
  5. Day 5: Bolzano – St. Moritz over the Passo dello Stelvio (48 hairpins), 190 km.
  6. Day 6: St. Moritz – Zermatt via Andermatt and the Furkapass, 250 km.
  7. Day 7: Rack-railway day up to Gornergrat, facing the Matterhorn.
  8. Day 8: Zermatt – Geneva through the Valais and Montreux, 240 km.

Recommended hotels by area

Unsponsored: these are the ones that have most surprised our testers for location, service and breakfast.

  • Chamonix: the B&Bs in Les Praz, 3 km from the centre, offer views of the Aiguille Verte at half the price of a hotel downtown.
  • Cortina d’Ampezzo: rifugi converted into small hotels (Rifugio Lagazuoi, Rifugio Averau) combine experience and savings.
  • St. Moritz: to avoid resort prices, stay in Pontresina or Silvaplana. Same lake, half the rate.
  • Zermatt: cars are banned in the village; leave the vehicle in Täsch (covered parking) and take the local train (12 min). Family hotels in Täsch come out 40 % cheaper than their Zermatt equivalents.

Practical tips

  • Swiss vignette: 40 CHF a year for motorways. Buy online or at the border.
  • Austrian vignette: 10-day pass for €12.80 if you cross Austria (western Tyrol).
  • Mont Blanc Tunnel: €55 one way in a car (2026); return tickets are cheaper if you come back within 7 days.
  • Chains or M+S tyres: mandatory in Switzerland from November to April and recommended on any high col even in June.
  • Fuel: prices vary a lot between Switzerland (high), Italy (medium) and France (medium-low). Fill up before entering Switzerland if you can.

Restaurants on the way

A long lunch at an alpine rifugio is part of the experience. Highlights: Val Gardena and Alta Badia offer Ladin cuisine fused with Italian and Austrian; the Aosta Valley has fondue au bleu d’Aoste and creamy polenta; the Valais is raclette country. Book ahead for rifugi accessible by road, especially on August market Tuesdays.
